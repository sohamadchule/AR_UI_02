AKRUTI AR - HONEST COMPARISON WITH THE MARKET
2026-10-26
=============================================

Why this exists: AR placement was built and tested on a phone, and failed in
ways a customer would notice at once (pieces kilometres wide, invisible, always
"overlapping"). Before building more, this compares what the companies already
doing this ship, and how they get their accuracy, with where Akruti actually
is.

A note on the evidence: most accuracy claims in this market are the companies'
own marketing (e.g. IKEA's "98%"). None of them publishes measured error in cm.
Where something is a vendor claim, it is marked as one.


1. WHO DOES THIS, AND WHAT THEY SHIP
------------------------------------

IKEA (Place, 2017; now Kreativ inside the IKEA app)
  - Place: true-scale AR on ARKit, later ARCore. IKEA claimed "98% scale
    accuracy" (its own figure, from 2017).
  - Kreativ: on LiDAR iPhones/iPads it scans the room into a 3D copy. You can
    erase your existing furniture and fill the room with IKEA products.
    Phones without LiDAR fall back to a photo-based wide-angle scan, which
    reviewers say is less reliable for distances.
  - Third-party guidance treats it as an inspiration / fit-check tool, "not a
    measurement tool": verify dimensions before buying.

Wayfair (View in Room 3D, Interactive Photo, Room Planner)
  - Several products in one room; LiDAR + RealityKit version on iOS.
  - Real-time lighting and shadows from the room's light; body occlusion
    (a person can stand in front of a virtual sofa).
  - Design from a saved photo of the room, away from home.
  - Claims products are "accurately sized to true dimensions" (own claim).
    Many features launched on iOS first.

Amazon (View in Your Room / Room Decorator, 2020)
  - Several products in one room, rearrangeable; add to cart without leaving AR.
  - Rooms saved to the account ("Your Rooms") and reopened from a saved image.
  - Built on ARKit; Android came later than iOS.

Houzz (View in My Room 3D)
  - Several products, on top of rugs; wall and ceiling items (pendant lights),
    lamps on tables.
  - Uses the ARCore Depth API to place furniture more accurately (per Google).
  - A tile tool walks you to each corner of the room to set its outline
    (the same idea as our "measure room by its corners").
  - An early reviewer noted the scene didn't stay anchored when the phone
    moved: even big players shipped stability problems.

3D Cloud (formerly Marxent): B2B platform for retailers (La-Z-Boy, Lowe's...)
  - Patented multi-product WebAR in the browser, a room planner, and
    configurable products (sectionals).
  - Main point: THEY MAKE THE 3D MODELS. They handle content creation, QA and
    WebAR conversion from the retailer's 2D images or CAD. Turnaround about
    6-8 weeks per launch.

Avataar (India; worked with Pepperfry), GlamAR (Fynd, India), Cylindo
  - Make 3D models from existing catalogue photos at scale, then offer
    true-to-scale AR in the browser or app. These are vendor claims.
  - This is the closest competition to Akruti's market (Indian furniture
    retail), and their selling point is the content pipeline, not the AR
    viewer.

Shopify (the de facto standard for merchants' 3D models)
  - A model must be "built to real-world scale" in the correct units. The
    test is: placed in AR next to the real product, "they should be the same
    size".
  - Origin centred at the product's base.
  - About 4 MB total; textures no larger than 2048 x 2048 for mobile web.
  - The merchant reviews every model against the real product before it goes
    live.


2. WHERE THEIR ACCURACY ACTUALLY COMES FROM
-------------------------------------------
  a) Correct 3D models. Every serious player treats the model as a
     quality-checked asset: built at real size from the product's
     specification, the origin at the base, a size budget, and a human check
     against the real product. Accuracy is mostly decided BEFORE the customer
     opens AR.
  b) Better sensors where available. LiDAR (iPhone Pro) for room scans;
     ARCore Depth API on Android for occlusion and placement.
  c) Honest positioning. Treated as a visual fit check, not a tape measure.
  d) Native apps for the hardest features (room scanning, saved rooms,
     occlusion, lighting), with WebAR for reach.

Nobody in these sources publishes measured centimetre error, and nobody was
found shipping furniture-to-furniture collision warnings as a product feature
(only patents and a research app). Our overlap and walking-space checks would
be different from the competition, but only if the sizes underneath are right.

4. WHERE WE ARE LAGGING: HONESTLY
---------------------------------
1. CONTENT, NOT CODE, IS THE BIGGEST GAP.
   Competitors start from QA'd, real-size models. We accept any GLB:
     - 5 of 12 live products were drawn kilometres wide in AR
     - 6 of 12 models are generic Google Poly shapes that are not the products
     - one product has an impossible listed size (10 x 10 x 10 cm desk)
     - the sofa is 13 MB / 282,000 triangles, about 3x the usual budget
       (about 4 MB)
   No runtime code can make a wrong model accurate. The AR code can only
   detect it and say so, which it now does.

2. WE BUILT THE ADVANCED FEATURES BEFORE THE BASICS WERE PROVEN.
   Overlap checks, room measuring and walkway warnings were built on top of
   wrong sizes, so they produced constant false "overlap" errors. The order
   should have been: correct models -> one product placed and measured on a
   phone -> several products -> checks.

3. MY OWN DESIGN MISTAKE. The rule "if the model doesn't match the listed
   size, show the file's own size" was wrong. For files in absurd units it
   put a 687 km desk in the room: invisible, and overlapping everything.
   Fixed (see section 6).

4. NO PHONE TESTING LOOP. The logic was tested on a PC only. Competitors
   ship after device testing. We have no device list, no measured error
   numbers, and no on-screen debug readout.

5. NO MODEL PIPELINE FOR SHOPKEEPERS. Our customers are furniture shops that
   don't have 3D models. Avataar, GlamAR, Cylindo and 3D Cloud win by making
   the models for them (from photos or CAD). Akruti assumes the shop brings a
   correct GLB. That is the biggest business gap.

6. ANDROID WEB HAS REAL CEILINGS. Google's own table for Chrome's web AR:
   hit test, anchors, depth and lighting estimation are SHIPPED; plane
   detection is only a DEVELOPER TRIAL; instant placement is NOT AVAILABLE.
   Most Android phones have no LiDAR, so a 3D room scan like IKEA Kreativ's
   isn't possible in the browser. Guided corner-tapping (what Houzz does for
   tiles) is the realistic way. We aren't behind here; it's the platform.

7. NOT YET USED, BUT AVAILABLE TO US: Chrome already ships lighting
   estimation (realistic shading) and the Depth API (occlusion, a real chair
   hiding the virtual sofa). Wayfair and Houzz use these; we don't yet.

8. MISSING CUSTOMER FEATURES competitors have: saving and sharing a room
   layout, add-to-cart from inside AR, and an iPhone path for several
   products.


5. WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
----------------------
 1. Fix the catalogue data now (no code): correct the "Desk" product (size and
    category); replace or remove the 6 generic demo models; compress the sofa
    to under 5 MB.
 2. Quality gate at upload (admin): refuse or warn when the model's shape
    doesn't match the entered size by more than 5%, when the size is
    implausible, or when the file is over about 5 MB / 150k triangles. Show the
    admin the AR preview at the real size before publishing.
 3. Prove one product on real phones: tape-measure test, write down the
    error, 3 phones. Add a hidden debug overlay (tracking state, floor
    height, model size) for testing.
 4. Only then re-test several products, overlaps and the room tools on
    phones.
 5. Realism: turn on lighting estimation; add depth occlusion on phones that
    support it.
 6. Customer features: save and share a layout, add to cart from AR.
 7. Business: a model-creation service or partner for shops without 3D models
    (the gap that Avataar / GlamAR / 3D Cloud fill).


6. FIXED TODAY (after the phone screenshot)
-------------------------------------------
  - Models are never drawn at a nonsense size any more:
      shape matches the listing   -> real size ("real size")
      shape differs               -> as close to the listing as its shape
                                     allows, with a notice giving the listed
                                     size and how far off it is (e.g.
                                     "differs by up to 20%")
      no size listed              -> the file's size, corrected by a power of
                                     ten if its units are clearly wrong
                                     (km / mm), with a notice
      still unbelievable (> 6 m or < 5 cm) -> refused, with a message
  - The product drawer marks products whose size is "approximate" or "not
    listed" before you add them.
  - Live catalogue result: all 12 products now come out at believable sizes
    (e.g. Kaam desk 140 x 82 x 71 cm instead of 687 km).
